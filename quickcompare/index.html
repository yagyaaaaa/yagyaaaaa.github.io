How a live version would work

From your list to your doorstep

QuickCompare would organize the shopping. Each store would still pack, charge for, and deliver its own order.

  1. You pick an address and add items

    Choose Home or Office once. We ask the stores what they can deliver there and compare the same product and pack size wherever available.

  2. We check the real cost before ordering

    See each store’s item prices, fees, and delivery estimate. If a price changes before checkout, you see the new amount before confirming.

  3. Each store handles its delivery

    Your basket can become several orders at the same address. You can see which orders went through and which need attention.

Say you want breakfast delivered every morning

Pick paneer, curd, and eggs. Choose 8–9 am, every day, and set a ₹300 limit. Before each delivery, QuickCompare checks stock, price, timing, and permission to order. If the basket is over ₹300 or a store needs you to sign in again, the affected order goes on hold. The delivery window is a preference; the store gives the actual estimate.

Why use two computers behind the scenes?

One keeps the app responsive

It shows your basket, remembers your schedule, and updates the screen as prices and delivery times come back.

One talks to the stores

It does the heavier work of checking stores and submitting approved orders. If 100 of 10,000 people are shopping, it handles the active requests and any deliveries due. People who aren’t shopping don’t each need a browser running.

Connecting a store would require its permission. The live service would protect saved connection details and let you disconnect a store. COD and prepaid would depend on what that store supports.

A QuickCompare subscription could cover price comparisons and repeat deliveries. Each store would still set its own prices, fees, and free-delivery minimum.

Technical details

The smaller VPS serves the app API and passes work through a queue. The larger VPS runs containerized jobs using approved provider APIs or isolated, provider-permitted browser sessions that close after use. Postgres stores addresses, schedules and order status. Permitted session data is encrypted with separately managed keys. Each store order has a unique attempt ID so a retry can be checked before placing a duplicate.
